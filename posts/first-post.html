Why I’m starting this blog

December 16, 2025

I’m starting this blog because I’m learning computer science from scratch, and I don’t want to pretend I know more than I do.

I recently came across this fantastic article by Julia Evans, and it made me realize how rare that kind of transparency actually is—especially in tech. So much of what we see online now is either hyper-curated success or quiet prestige signaling (I’ve spent my fair share of time doom-scrolling LinkedIn), rather than a candid look at the learning process.

This blog is my attempt to do the opposite.

For some background: I came into UC San Diego as a Political Science major, fully intending to follow the pre-law track. In high school, I gravitated much more toward the humanities and social sciences than STEM. Both of my parents have held very different careers—my mom is a patent lawyer, my dad is a software engineer—and while my dad made many sincere attempts to get me into coding (HTML lessons in third grade, coding camps, side projects), I hated it. Truly.

Me, obviously more of a reader than a coder! I also went to a high school with a highly competitive STEM culture (we literally had a “math wars” rivalry my senior year), which only reinforced the idea that technical fields were for people who had started early and were already exceptional. I decided, pretty confidently, that this just wasn’t my thing. Last year, though, I took COGS 9 (otherwise known as Introduction to Data Science for _) with Professor Dominic Burrows which completely changed my life. I still remember walking out of the first lecture was talking about the human brain project—a ten-year long EU program with billion invested that ended up being a massive failure. and literally calling my mom and saying this is the coolest class i’ve ever taken. Funny enough, I realized a quarter later that I was actually not meant to take this class at all, had actually gotten it mixed up with one of my requirements and _. but _. A big reason why STEM didn’t appeal to me in the past was because it was too granular. For example, chemistry even though it’s of course like life like chemistry is all around us it’s hard to see how knowing elements of the periodic table has to do with everyday life, or how it connects. But data science is so applied, and using your brain in such a logical STEMMy way while solving to quirky problems—like, (insert example here) It still took me a while to seriously consider transitioning. Law school had been my plan for years, and I wasn’t sure a technical path made sense for me academically. Once I saw that I could keep up (and even enjoy it!) the hesitation started to fade. At the same time, I began feeling like my Political Science classes weren’t rigorous in the ways I wanted, which ultimately led me to change direction. This blog is a place to document that process: the learning curve, the mistakes, the confusion, and the small wins. It’s also a space for occasional life updates—something for future me to look back on and remember what it felt like to be figuring things out in college. I want this to stand as proof that you can start late, pivot paths, and still build something meaningful. Especially in tech, it can feel like everyone else has been coding since childhood.
